Another day dealing with the perpetual problem of monster preservation. I'm running out of storage space for all these specimens, and having them rot in my warehouse isn't much better than letting them decay in the field. Tittlin suggested getting Raffn'r Jacobsen to teleport materials back, but where am I supposed to get enough ice for proper preservation? I'd need tons of it just to keep everything fresh long enough to extract useful components.

The thought occurred to me - perhaps I could replicate whatever magic preserved the Jade Lady in that ghost's cottage. She was kept from aging artificially for who knows how long. With my necromancy skills, I might be able to work out something similar to extend the usefulness of these monster parts. Unfortunately, she wasn't the practitioner, just the victim, so asking her for technical details would be pointless.

Tittlin eventually convinced me to just focus on the hunt at hand rather than trying to solve every logistical problem at once. We checked the adventure board in Fronzgan and found a few options - an ogre problem, something about a water supply, and a noble requesting a dragon breath pouch from a young white dragon. The dragon quest caught my attention immediately.

I checked my laboratory inventory and confirmed I have the essences needed for dragon resurrection work - fire, earth, and water elementals all accounted for. No air essence yet, but that's fine. The white dragon's breath pouch would be useful, and honestly, we could use the practice before tackling that ice dragon situation that's been looming over us.

The others debated between the quests while I worked on copying some spell scrolls. I managed to transcribe Protection from Evil and Good during our downtime - two hours per spell level, 300 gold pieces in materials consumed. The precision work helps me think.

We decided on the water supply investigation initially, but then pivoted to the white dragon hunt since Tittlin could handle underwater threats with his magical gear. I stayed behind to continue my research, connecting via Rary's Telepathic Bond to observe through their eyes while I worked in my laboratory. A productive use of time, really - they could handle reconnaissance while I made progress on my theoretical work.

Of course, they ended up finding not one but two young white dragons. I tried to determine if white dragons have resistance to fire or vulnerability to it, given their cold nature, but my nature check was abysmal. A three. Absolutely embarrassing for someone who's supposed to be a dragon expert. Even with my proficiency bonus applied, I only managed a nine total. All I could confirm was that white dragons are resistant to cold damage - hardly groundbreaking research.

After a long rest, I prepared my spells carefully. Dropped Leomund's Tiny Hut since we wouldn't need it, swapped out Greater Invisibility for more practical options. I spent five hours copying spell scrolls and another seven on Protection from Evil and Good. The mathematics of spell preparation is soothing in its predictability, unlike the chaos of actual adventuring.

We hired Gerd the healer to accompany Spesof and Tittlin, and I asked them to check with Ecstasy about dragon-slaying arrows. He didn't have any in stock - those specialty items cost 5,000 gold pieces each apparently. We made do with what we had.

The cave system was extensive, filled with perhaps thirty to forty giant spiders in addition to the two young white dragons. The tactical situation was complex. We debated strategies for nearly an hour - using Compulsion to drive one dragon away, employing Wall of Force for protection, various combinations of invisibility and stealth.

I consulted my dragon bones with Augury twice, asking about our chances. The portents were mixed but not disastrous. We settled on a bold plan: I would have Gerd cast Earthquake to collapse part of the cave while I maintained a Wall of Force to protect us from falling debris. The goal was to trap one dragon, kill it quickly, and hope the adult dragons didn't return immediately.

The execution was chaotic but effective. Gerd's Earthquake brought tons of rock down on the cave. We maintained our protective barrier while engaging the isolated young dragon. Ignis managed to grapple it despite being smaller than these specimens - young white dragons are larger than I'd estimated. The creature's cold breath weapon dealt significant damage, fifty points of cold to Ignis, half that to the rest of us.

Spesof and Tittlin made short work of it once it was pinned. The thing kept burning from Tittlin's enchanted arrows even after death, which was somewhat concerning from a specimen preservation standpoint.

Here's where it gets frustrating. When it came time to harvest the dragon, Gerd simply stepped forward and efficiently extracted everything - the breath pouch for the noble, prime cuts of meat, blood samples, bones, scales, and wing membranes. She cast Enhance Ability on herself for advantage on the delicate work and proceeded to show me up at my own supposed area of expertise.

I'm a dragon researcher. I've spent years studying their anatomy, their resurrection, their essential nature. And yet a field cleric with some basic anatomical knowledge and the right preparation spells outperformed me completely. The game mechanics don't quite match the backstory I've built for Momen, and I'm not sure what the solution is. Perhaps I need to invest in more practical harvesting skills rather than just theoretical knowledge.

We secured the dragon corpse with Gentle Repose and made our escape before the adult dragons returned. They'd apparently abandoned their offspring entirely - fled without even attempting to retrieve the body. Cold-blooded in more ways than one, I suppose.

The loot was decent, and we completed the contract, but I'm left feeling somewhat inadequate in my own specialty. Something to work on, I suppose, along with the dragon restoration research and the perpetual storage problem.

At least the spell copying went well. That's something.
